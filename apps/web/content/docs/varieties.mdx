---
title: Varieties and registers
description: Town and regional forms, and how formal a word is.
---

A language is not one fixed thing. Town Bemba and Town Nyanja borrow heavily from English and
differ from rural forms, and Malawian Chichewa differs from Zambian Nyanja. Rather than pick one
and drop the rest, the API tags each entry.

## Varieties

Each language has a standard variety: Zambian Nyanja for Nyanja, for example. An entry from
another variety carries a `variety` code, such as a town or regional form; entries of the
standard variety have none.

To get only one variety, pass `variety` to search and lookup:

```bash
curl -H "X-API-Key: $VERNACULAR_KEY" \
  "$VERNACULAR_URL/v1/entries/search?language=nya&q=madzi&variety=town"
```

Without `variety`, every variety is returned and you can read each entry's tag.

## Registers

`register` says where a word fits socially: `neutral`, `formal`, `informal`, `town`,
`respectful`, `vulgar` or `archaic`. Most words are `neutral`. Check it before showing a word to
the public; a `respectful` form may be expected when addressing elders.
