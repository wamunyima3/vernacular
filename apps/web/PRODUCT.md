# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two primary audiences, equally important:

- **Developers** building software for Zambians: government and public-service teams, and health, agriculture and finance organisations. They come to the portal to judge whether the data can be trusted, read the docs, get an API key and watch their usage.
- **Volunteer native speakers** who contribute words, phrases and examples, and the reviewers and senior reviewers who check them. They work in the signed-in tools (contribute, review, disputes, re-checks), often on mid-range Android phones over costly mobile data.

Learners and researchers also browse entries; admins run approvals and sources.

## Product Purpose

An open API and dataset of human-verified words, phrases and translations for Zambian languages: Bemba and Nyanja first, then Tonga and Lozi, then Kaonde, Lunda and Luvale. Success is that a team building for Zambians can rely on the data, and that the volunteers who make it want to keep going.

## Positioning

Nothing is marked verified until two native speakers have accepted it, and every item carries its sources, licences and who checked it. It is built and owned by the Zambian community that speaks these languages: volunteers are credited by name, local partners supply sources, and the data is published openly. Machine translation is always labelled as machine output and never presented as verified.

## Operating Context

- Developers read the docs, try queries on /try, create keys and call the API from their own code.
- Contributors add items with a licence agreement; reviewers decide blind and in pairs; senior reviewers settle disputes and run quarterly re-checks.
- Releases are versioned dataset snapshots that developers can pin.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Trust statuses: verified, reviewed, sourced, machine, disputed. Their meaning is fixed by the API contract.
- Pages must work on phones and on low bandwidth: little JavaScript, no heavy media.
- Interface language: undecided. It is English today; whether to localise the interface is open.
- Machine translation is subject to the Gemma Terms of Use, as set out at /docs/terms.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Vernacular.
- Voice: plain, factual and specific. Say who checked a word and where it came from; avoid hype and claims about coverage that the data does not support.
- No logo or other brand assets exist yet.

## Evidence on Hand

- Live counts from the API, per language (for example Bemba: 2,843 words, of which 0 are verified at the time of writing).
- The contributor credits page lists real volunteers by name.
- No testimonials, partner logos, case studies or benchmark results exist yet; do not invent them. Benchmark scores come later (task P3-05b).

## Product Principles

1. Trust is shown, not claimed: every item says how far it has been checked and by whom.
2. Credit the community: the people who speak the languages own and are named in the work.
3. Credible enough for public-sector adoption: calm, precise and accountable.
4. Light enough for a phone on mobile data.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is required. Many users are on small screens with costly data; names and words in Zambian languages must render correctly.
